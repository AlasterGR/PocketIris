THE FOUR WAYS TO LINK THE PHONE TO THIS PC (read this first)
==============================================================
  1. USB cable            Nothing to set up. Fastest.
  2. Wi-Fi                Phone and PC on the same Wi-Fi. No Bluetooth, no Windows click.
  3. Wi-Fi + Bluetooth    The traffic goes over the Bluetooth network; the phone must also be on some Wi-Fi. One Windows click.
  4. Bluetooth only       Bluetooth network only. Needs adb's classic port 5555, which the PC script opens through the USB cable
                          once after every phone restart.

How to use any of them (no commands to remember)
  Phone:  PocketIris tab > Developer > "Connect to another device by" > pick the mode.
          It asks Allow/Deny and then your lock screen. It is always Off again after the app restarts. "Off" restores everything.
  PC:     the background watcher opens a PowerShell window by itself and connects. If you prefer: double-click the Desktop shortcut
          "PocketIris Link" and press 1 (Wi-Fi), 2 (Wi-Fi + Bluetooth), 3 (Bluetooth only) or 4 (Off, back to USB).
  Under the hood: tools\phone-link.ps1 -Mode wifi|wifibt|bt [-Smoke]   (bt-debug.ps1 is the old name for the Wi-Fi + Bluetooth mode)

Status: all four were tested end to end on the POCO F5 (smoke test 8/8). Bluetooth only was proven with no cable AND the phone's
Wi-Fi switched off (the phone itself reported Wi-Fi off, adb was connected to the Bluetooth address on port 5555).
Note for Bluetooth only without Wi-Fi: the doorbell travels over Wi-Fi, so with Wi-Fi off the PC watcher cannot hear it. Double-click
the Desktop shortcut "PocketIris Link" and press 3 instead.
The rest of this file describes the Bluetooth parts in detail.

DEBUGGING THE PHONE OVER BLUETOOTH (no cable)
==============================================

What it does
  One switch in the app and one command on the PC give the PC full adb access to the phone over Bluetooth: install, push, pull,
  logcat, shell, the page inspector (cdp.ps1) and the smoke test, exactly as over USB. It is slower than USB (a 5 MB file takes
  about 35 seconds) and Bluetooth range applies.

How it works (and why it is built this way)
  Bluetooth tethering gives the PC and the phone a small network over Bluetooth, and adb runs over any network ("Wireless
  debugging"). There is deliberately no custom Bluetooth bridge: Android's own authenticated adb is the only way in.

One-time setup
  1. Pair the phone with the PC (Windows Bluetooth settings), once.
  2. Grant the app its one permission, once (needs the USB cable the first time; on Xiaomi also switch on
     Developer options > "USB debugging (Security settings)"):
        adb shell pm grant io.github.alastergr.pocketiris android.permission.WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS
  3. Phone: switch on PocketIris's accessibility service (Android Settings > Accessibility > PocketIris phone control). The app
     needs it only to flip the Bluetooth tethering switch for you. Without it, switch tethering on yourself.

Easiest way (set up once, then nothing to remember)
  - On the phone, switch "Bluetooth debugging" On (Allow, then your lock check). That is all you do on the phone.
  - The PC notices by itself: the phone sends a small "doorbell" message over Wi-Fi, and a quiet background script on the PC
    (bt-watch.ps1, started at Windows sign-in) opens a PowerShell window and the Devices and Printers window.
  - Do Windows' one click (right-click the phone > Connect using > Access point). The window then finishes everything.
  - No Wi-Fi shared by phone and PC? Use the Desktop shortcut "PocketIris Bluetooth debugging" instead (double-click it after
    switching On). "PocketIris Bluetooth debugging OFF" goes back to USB.
  - To install the background watcher on a new PC: double-click tools\Install-Bluetooth-Watcher.cmd. To remove it: delete
    "PocketIris Bluetooth watcher" from the Startup folder (Win+R, type shell:startup). The first time, Windows Firewall asks
    whether PowerShell may receive messages: choose Allow.
  - The doorbell is only the text "pocketiris-btdebug-on" and can do nothing except start bt-debug.ps1 on your PC.

The same thing by hand
Every time
  Phone:  PocketIris tab > Developer > "Connect to another device by" > Bluetooth.
          It asks Allow/Deny, then Android's own lock check (fingerprint, PIN or pattern). It then switches on Wireless debugging and
          Bluetooth tethering by itself, finds the debugging port, and starts answering the PC on its status port (3499, reachable only
          from the Bluetooth network). It is always off again after the app restarts. Keep the phone awake while connecting.
  PC:     tools\bt-debug.ps1 -Smoke
          Windows needs ONE click: a Devices and Printers window opens; right-click the phone > Connect using > Access point. The
          script waits up to 90 seconds, then carries on by itself: it asks the phone which port to use, connects adb, remembers the
          connection for cdp.ps1 and smoke-test.ps1, and runs the smoke test.
  Done:   bt-debug.ps1 -Off on the PC, and switch "Bluetooth debugging" Off on the phone. Off puts Wireless debugging back to how it
          was and switches tethering off again if the switch was what turned it on.

Why the one Windows click
  Windows has no supported command to join a phone's Bluetooth network. We tried its Bluetooth functions: they claim success but do
  nothing (and can break the menu entry). The click is the reliable way.

Good to know
  - The Wireless debugging port changes every time Android restarts that feature; the switch finds the new one and tells the PC, so
    you never type it.
  - The Bluetooth link can drop when the phone sleeps; wake the phone and join again.
  - Everything is also available by hand: the PocketIris tab > Developer has shortcuts for Bluetooth, Tethering and Developer options, and a
    "Network addresses and debugging port" self-test.

Safety
  - The switch is off by default, off after every app restart, and turning it on needs Allow plus your lock screen.
  - Only a PC whose adb key you approved on the phone can connect (Android asks once: "Allow debugging?").
  - While it is on, anyone with such a PC can do almost anything on the phone. Switch it off when you are done.
